He stopped and knockedknocked /nɒkt/ (v. past): struck a door or surface with one's knuckles to attract attention on the door. An old woman answered. The woman explained to him that the children had been crying for food since morning. She had pretendedpretended /prɪˈtendɪd/ (v. past): behaved as if something were true when it was not to cook by boiling water to pacifypacify /ˈpæsɪfaɪ/ (v.): to make someone calm and peaceful; to quiet down them and give them hope.

Umar was deeply moved and felt ashamedashamed /əˈʃeɪmd/ (adj.): feeling embarrassed or guilty about something. It was an exciting day! First, we had a special assembly. Then, the principal was giving a speech when the lights suddenly went off.

Grammar — Past Simple & Past Continuous
8 Marks

📌 Grammar Rules

TenseFormUseExample
Past Simpleverb + -ed (or irregular)Completed action at a specific timeUmar walked to the hut.
Past Continuouswas/were + verb-ingAction in progress at a time in the pastThe children were crying loudly.
CombinedPast cont. + when/while + past simpleLonger action interrupted by shorter oneShe was cooking when Umar knocked.

💡 Remember: while is used with past continuous; when is usually used with past simple to show an interruption.

Reading II — Sakela Sili
6 Marks

Pre-reading: Do you know any traditional Nepali dances or festivals?

Sakela Sili is one of the most important and beautiful festivals of the KiratKirat /kɪˈrɑːt/ (n.): an ancient indigenous ethnic group of Nepal living mainly in the hills of eastern Nepal community of Nepal. It is celebrated twice a year — once in summer (Ubhauli) and once in winter (Udhauli). The festival is a way of expressing gratitude to nature for a good harvest and praying for the safety of the people.

During Sakela, the Rai and Limbu communities gathergather /ˈɡæðər/ (v.): to come together in a group; to collect at the local Sakela stoneSakela stone (n.): a sacred stone worshipped by the Kirat community during the Sakela festival for worship. They performed special dances called silisili (n.): the traditional dance performed during the Sakela festival by the Kirat people, which imitateimitate /ˈɪmɪteɪt/ (v.): to copy the actions or movements of someone or something the movements of animals, birds, farming activities and nature. Each movement tells a story about life, work, and the natural world.

The dances were performed in large groups, with men and women dancing together. The main dancer is called the NachowangNachowang (n.): the lead dancer who guides the group during the Sakela Sili dance, who leads all the other dancers. Traditional instruments such as the dhol and jhyamta provided the rhythmrhythm /ˈrɪðəm/ (n.): a strong regular repeated pattern of sound or movement for the dance.

Sakela Sili is not just a festival. It is a living tradition that keeps the culture, history and identity of the Kirat people alivealive /əˈlaɪv/ (adj.): living; continuing to exist. Today, Sakela has been declared a public holiday in Nepal, showing how important this festival is for the national heritageheritage /ˈherɪtɪdʒ/ (n.): traditions, achievements and beliefs that are part of the history of a group of Nepal.

🔤 What are Consonants?

The English alphabet has 26 letters. Of these, 5 are vowels (A, E, I, O, U) and the remaining 21 are consonants.

Consonants are sounds made by blocking or restricting the air flow from the mouth in some way. They are usually combined with vowels to form syllables and words.
