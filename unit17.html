Pre-reading:

a. Have you ever visited a palm reader or fortune teller?   b. Do you believe in fate?

Once upon a time, there was a young man named Binod who lived in a small village near Pokhara. One day, Binod had gone to the market to sell some vegetables when he noticednoticed /ˈnəʊtɪst/ (v. past): saw or became aware of something a large crowd gathered around an old man. The old man was reading the palms of people and telling them their future.

Binod was curiouscurious /ˈkjʊəriəs/ (adj.): eager to know or learn something; inquisitive and waited in the queue. When his turn came, the palm reader took his hand and studied the lines carefully. After a long silence, the old man spoke in a gravegrave /ɡreɪv/ (adj.): serious and worrying; giving cause for concern voice.

"Young man," he said, "I see that you will face a great misfortunemisfortune /mɪsˈfɔːtʃən/ (n.): bad luck; an unfortunate condition or event before you turn thirty. But after that, great happiness awaits you."

Binod was shocked. He had never expected such news. He had plannedplanned /plænd/ (v. past): had arranged or intended to do something in advance to marry his childhood sweetheart and open a small shop. Now, all those dreams seemed uncertain. He returned home in silencesilence /ˈsaɪləns/ (n.): complete absence of sound; the state of being quiet.

Days turned into weeks, and Binod could not shake offshake off (phrasal v.): to get rid of; to free oneself from a feeling or situation the fear. He had stopped talking to friends and had withdrawnwithdrawn /wɪðˈdrɔːn/ (adj./v. past part.): having pulled back or removed oneself from a situation from social life. His mother noticed the change and asked him what had happened.

When Binod told his mother, she laughed gently. "My son," she said, "you have forgottenforgotten /fəˈɡɒtən/ (v. past part.): failed to remember; ceased to keep in mind the most important thing. Our future is not written in our palms — it is shaped by our actions. No one can predictpredict /prɪˈdɪkt/ (v.): to say what will happen in the future; to forecast your destiny."

Binod felt as if a heavy burden had been lifted from his shoulders. He realisedrealised /ˈrɪəlaɪzd/ (v. past): became fully aware of or understood something that he had wasted many days worrying about something uncertain. From that day on, he lived life with courage, and as the years passed, he prosperedprospered /ˈprɒspəd/ (v. past): became successful; grew and developed well greatly — not because of fate, but because of his hard work and determination.

Grammar — Past Perfect Tense
8 Marks

📌 Past Perfect — Rules

FormUseExample
had + past participleAction completed BEFORE another past actionBy the time I arrived, she had left.
Negative: had not (hadn't) + ppSomething had not happened before another past eventHe hadn't eaten when she called.
Question: Had + subject + pp?Asking about action before another past eventHad you finished before he came?

💡 Key words: before, after, already, just, never, by the time, when, once

Timeline: Past Perfect ← → Past Simple ← → Now
"He had slept when she arrived." — sleeping happened first ✅

⏳ Choose above
2. She was angry because she _______ (wait) for two hours.
⏳ Choose above
3. I _______ (never/visit) Chitwan before last year.
⏳ Choose above

⏳ Choose above

B. Join sentences using Past Perfect (4 marks)

Example: She ate dinner. Then her friend arrived. → She had eaten dinner before her friend arrived.

1. Binod went to market. Then he saw the palm reader. (by the time)

2. The players ran after a ball. They entered the ground first. (after)

3. She studied hard. She passed the exam. (because)

4. I lost my book. I could not do homework. (since)

🔤 What are Vowels?

There are 5 vowels in the English alphabet: A, E, I, O, U. Vowels are sounds made with an open mouth — the air flows freely without being blocked.
