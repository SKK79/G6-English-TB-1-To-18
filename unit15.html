It is famous for its lushlush /lʌʃ/ (adj.): growing thickly and strongly; very green and healthy green tea gardens, rolling hills, and pleasant climate. But Ilam is not just famous for tea. It is also home to some of Nepal's most talentedtalented /ˈtæləntɪd/ (adj.): having a natural ability to do something well folk musicians.

In the villages of Ilam, you may hear the sweet sounds of the sarangisarangi (n.): a traditional Nepali string instrument played with a bow, often associated with the Gandharba community, the madal, and the flute. These instruments are integralintegral /ˈɪntɪɡrəl/ (adj.): essential; necessary as a part of a whole to the culture of the people there. The Gandharba community, who are renownedrenowned /rɪˈnaʊnd/ (adj.): known and admired by many people; famous for their musical skills, have been playing and singing folk songs for generations.

One legendarylegendary /ˈledʒəndri/ (adj.): very famous and admired, especially from the past musician from Ilam was Chakra Bahadur. He could play the sarangi with extraordinaryextraordinary /ɪkˈstrɔːdɪnri/ (adj.): very unusual or remarkable; beyond what is ordinary skill. It is said that when he played, even the birds would stop singing to listen. His music told stories of love, nature, sorrow and joy. People from nearby villages would gathergather /ˈɡæðər/ (v.): to come together in a group; to collect around him to listen for hours.

Today, the traditiontradition /trəˈdɪʃən/ (n.): a custom or way of doing something that has been passed down through generations of folk music in Ilam may be at risk. Many young people are moving to cities and may not learn these traditional arts. However, some enthusiasticenthusiastic /ɪnˌθjuːziˈæstɪk/ (adj.): showing great excitement and interest groups are working to keep this tradition alive. They might open music schools and perhaps hold festivals to preservepreserve /prɪˈzɜːv/ (v.): to keep something in its original state or in good condition this beautiful heritage.

Perhaps one day, the music of Ilam will be known throughout the world. The musicians believe that music must be shared. It must not be allowed to disappear. As long as one person plays the sarangi in Ilam, the tradition will survivesurvive /səˈvaɪv/ (v.): to continue to live or exist, especially after danger.

Grammar — may / might / must (Probability)
8 Marks

📌 Grammar Rule: Expressing Degrees of Probability

ModalMeaningExample
may50% possible (more certain)It may rain this evening.
mightless certain possibilityShe might come tomorrow.
mustalmost certain (logical deduction)He studied hard. He must be tired.
perhaps / maybeadverbs of probabilityPerhaps she is at home.

💡 Remember: may > might (may is more certain). must here is about deduction, NOT obligation!

Reading II — Arrow and Song (Poem)
6 Marks

Pre-reading: When and why do we feel happy or sad?

Arrow and Song — H.W. Longfellow

I shot an arrow into the air,

It fell to earth, I knew not where;

For, so swiftly it flew, the sight

Could not follow it in its flight.


I breathed a song into the air,

It fell to earth, I knew not where;

For who has sight so keen and strong,

That it can follow the flight of song?


Long, long afterward, in an oak

I found the arrow, still unbroke;

And the song, from beginning to end,

I found again in the heart of a friend.

Extra Bit — may / might / must
5 Marks

🔍 Detailed Rules

ModalUseExample
mayPossibility (more likely ~50%)
Formal permission		It may be hot tomorrow.
May I come in?
mightPossibility (less certain ~30%)
Past form of may		She might be at school.
He said he might come.
mustStrong deduction / certainty
Obligation (different meaning)		He's been working hard. He must be tired.
You must wear a helmet.

⚠️ Note: When must is used for deduction (probability), it is different from when it is used for obligation. Context tells you which meaning is intended!
